Council Tax

The Necessary Evil

Council tax funds local services and is charged per property, not per person.

This means if you share a flat with 3 other people, the council tax amount is split 3 ways—not charged per person.

Student Relief

  • All full-time students → £0
  • Mixed households → discounts apply
  • Single occupant → 25% off

Important: You need to apply for student exemption. It doesn't happen automatically. Your university will provide a Council Tax Exemption Certificate.

How Much Does Council Tax Cost?

It varies wildly by:

  • Location (city/region)
  • Property band (size/value of property)
  • Local council decisions

Typical annual amounts:

  • Band A (smallest properties): £1,000-1,500
  • Band B: £1,200-1,800
  • Band C: £1,400-2,000
  • Band D (average): £1,600-2,300
  • Bands E-H (larger properties): £2,000-4,000+
Quick Check: Search "[your postcode] council tax band" to find out what you'll pay.

Who Has to Pay?

Council tax is the responsibility of the resident, not the landlord (unless specified in tenancy agreement).

In shared houses:

  • If you're all on the tenancy agreement → split the bill
  • If one person is the main tenant → they're legally responsible (but should split with housemates)
  • If it's an HMO (House in Multiple Occupation) → landlord might pay

Discounts & Exemptions

25% discount if:

  • You live alone
  • Only one adult lives there (other occupants are children under 18 or students)

50% discount if:

  • Everyone in the property is a full-time student
  • The property is an annex occupied by elderly/disabled relatives

100% exemption if:

  • Everyone is a full-time student
  • Property is unoccupied and unfurnished (time-limited)
  • Occupied only by people under 18
  • Owner is in prison or long-term care

Other discounts:

  • Disability reduction (if property is adapted for disabled person)
  • Severe mental impairment discount
  • Care leavers exemption (under 25, formerly in care)
⚠️ CRITICAL WARNING: Never ignore council tax letters. They escalate fast.

Ignoring council tax can lead to:
  • Court summons
  • Bailiff visits
  • Money taken directly from wages
  • County Court Judgement (CCJ) on your credit file
If you can't pay, contact the council immediately to arrange a payment plan. They're usually reasonable if you communicate.

Payment Options

Standard: 10 monthly payments (April-January)

Alternative: 12 monthly payments (April-March) for smaller amounts

Most councils strongly prefer Direct Debit. Some charge extra for other payment methods.

Common Council Tax Mistakes

Mistake 1: Not registering when you move in

Result: Bills pile up, late payment charges added

Fix: Register within a few days of moving in

Mistake 2: Assuming housemates will handle it

Result: Nobody does, everyone gets in trouble

Fix: Agree who's responsible and check it's actually being paid

Mistake 3: Not claiming student exemption

Result: Paying council tax when you shouldn't be

Fix: Apply as soon as you get your exemption certificate from uni

Mistake 4: Ignoring letters because you "don't understand them"

Result: Court summons, bailiffs, stress

Fix: Open all council tax letters. Call the council if confused. They'll explain.

Moving In/Out

Moving in:

  • Contact council within 21 days with move-in date
  • Provide names of all adults in property
  • Claim any discounts/exemptions immediately
  • Set up Direct Debit

Moving out:

  • Tell council your move-out date
  • Provide forwarding address
  • Get final bill and settle any balance
  • Don't assume the next tenant will sort it

What Does Council Tax Pay For?

Ever wondered where your money goes?

  • Rubbish collection and recycling
  • Street lighting and road maintenance
  • Libraries and leisure centers
  • Social care for elderly and vulnerable
  • Parks and public spaces
  • Local police and fire services (partial)
  • Schools (partial funding)
Reality Check: Council tax is one of those bills that feels annoying until you remember it keeps bins collected, streets lit, and libraries open. It's expensive, yes, but ignoring it is the worst possible move. Set up Direct Debit, claim your discounts, and forget about it.

Challenging Your Council Tax Band

Think your property is in the wrong band? You can challenge it if:

  • There's been a significant change to the property or area
  • You've just moved in and think it's wrong
  • Similar properties nearby are in lower bands

Check comparable properties on the Valuation Office Agency website. If you have a case, you can appeal. Be warned: your band can go UP as well as down if reassessed.

Pro Tip: In Scotland, council tax also includes water and sewerage charges, which simplifies billing but gives you less control over costs.

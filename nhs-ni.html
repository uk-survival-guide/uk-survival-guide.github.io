NHS Number & National Insurance Number

Two Numbers You’ll Need (Sooner Than You Think)

There are two very important numbers in the UK that quietly control your adult life:

  • NHS Number → healthcare
  • National Insurance (NI) Number → work, tax, and benefits

They sound boring.
They are not optional.

🩺 Registering with the NHS (Do This Before You’re Sick)

The NHS is free at the point of use — but only if you’re registered.

First step:
Register with a GP (General Practitioner) near where you live.

Do this as soon as you move in — not when you’re already ill and panicking.

Why?

  • GP registration can take days or weeks
  • Many practices are full
  • You can’t book most NHS appointments without being registered
Top Rule of UK Life:

👉 Register while healthy.
The system rewards planning, not emergencies.

Your NHS Number

Once registered, you’ll be assigned an NHS number — a 10-digit ID.

You’ll need it for:

  • Booking appointments
  • Prescriptions
  • Referrals
  • NHS App access

If you’ve lived in the UK before, you probably already have one.
It never changes.

Booking Appointments (Yes, It’s… Surprisingly Functional)

Most GP surgeries now use online booking systems.

Common platforms include:

  • NHS App (official)
  • Patient Access
  • PATCHS
  • Accurx

Some practices use newer booking platforms that are…
shockingly functional by UK standards.

You might even find one called something like “Bookable Health”, which feels:

  • Optimistic
  • Slightly ironic
  • Possibly a quiet apology for the previous system

Important NHS Tips (From People Who Learned the Hard Way)

  • Appointments are often released at 8am — refresh fast
  • Telephone appointments are normal (and often quicker)
  • Nurses can handle more than you think
  • A&E is for emergencies only — not a GP shortcut

🧾 National Insurance (NI) Number

Your Work & Tax Identity

Your NI number is used for:

  • Employment
  • Paying tax
  • Pension contributions
  • Benefits and student loans

You usually receive it:

  • Automatically if you grew up in the UK
  • By applying after arrival if you’re new

If you don’t have one yet:

  • You can still start work
  • But you must apply as soon as possible
  • Your employer will ask for it

How to Keep These Safe

Store the following in your “Boring Adult Stuff” folder:

  • NHS number
  • NI number
  • GP registration confirmation
  • Any NHS letters

You will be asked for these at the most random moments.

Final Healthcare Reality Check 💙

The NHS is not fast.
It is not fancy.
But it does work.

The people are kind, the system is overworked, and preparation makes everything smoother.

Register early.
Get the apps.
Future you will be very grateful.
